What can be done on this band? #

Propagation character #

2200 metres is the lowest-frequency amateur band, below medium wave, and propagates mainly by ground wave throughout the day; at night, as the ionospheric D layer weakens, the sky-wave contribution increases and the range widens. This band is suited to long-distance (DX) contacts using weak-signal techniques that require very narrow bandwidth, but daily variability is high and atmospheric noise (especially in the summer months) can make reception difficult.

Typical modes and activities #

The band uses almost exclusively CW and QRSS (very slow telegraphy) and narrowband digital modes; voice (phone) modes are impractical, because both transmitter power and antenna efficiency are low at these frequencies. Amateurs usually work with small loops or shortened antennas, and weak-signal digital modes (WSPR-like techniques) are common.

Spot frequencies #

No separate centre of activity (spot) frequency is defined for this band.

Notes specific to Turkey #

The band is allocated to class A amateurs in Turkey; other classes cannot operate in this band. The TR table gives an output power limit for this band and the emission types are listed separately; for details see the TR legal table section of the page.
