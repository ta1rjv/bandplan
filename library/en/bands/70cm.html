Licence class access #

  • Class A: Whole band (subject to the table conditions)
  • Class B: Whole band (subject to the table conditions)
  • Class C*: 430–440 MHz, 5 W

* This class's access is limited to a sub-range narrower than the whole band; appearing in the coarse class list does not mean full-band access.

Class B and C operators, while operating under the responsible operator's supervision at a club, school, or research station, or at a class A station, may use ALL table frequencies and emission types. Outside that context, they may only use the frequencies permitted for their own class.

What can be done on this band? #

Propagation character #

70 centimetres, like 2 metres, is a UHF band largely limited to line-of-sight; reflection/diffraction effects from buildings and obstacles are more pronounced than on 2 metres, which makes urban repeater use valuable. Under tropospheric ducting conditions the range can extend significantly; Earth-Moon-Earth (EME) techniques are also used on this band for weak-signal DX work.

Typical modes and activities #

The band contains input/output channel blocks reserved for different regional repeater systems (such as the F/PA/ON system and the Region 1 system, with different frequency shifts), an all-mode segment for CW/SSB/telegraphy, a beacon segment, a wide section for the satellite service and digital ATV (amateur television), and also FM/digital voice simplex channels.

Spot frequencies #

432200 kHz is the SSB centre of activity, 433500 kHz is the FM calling channel (channel designator U280), and 437000 kHz is the digital ATV/data centre of activity.

Notes specific to Turkey #

The band is open to class A, B and C amateurs in Turkey; class C licence holders may operate with at most 5 W output power. The TR allocation is not a single range but the union of several separate sub-bands (430.200-430.700, 431.550-431.825, 432.000-432.975, 433.400-433.575, 435.000-437.975 and 439.150-439.425 MHz); the sub-bands 431.550-431.825 and 439.150-439.425 MHz are reserved for repeater equipment installed by amateur radio associations. The 432 MHz region is also used for amateur-satellite and EME communication; the maximum transmitter output power for handheld radios in FM mode is 5 W, and the maximum equipment output power of beacons is 25 W.
