What can be done on this band? #

Propagation character #

160 metres ("Top Band") is a classic night-time band: during the day, D-layer absorption largely extinguishes signals and range stays local or regional; at night, once the D layer disappears, intercontinental distances can open through F-layer reflections. Winter months and periods of low solar noise generally give better DX conditions; atmospheric noise (especially during summer thunderstorm periods) is a significant limiting factor.

Typical modes and activities #

The lower part of the band is reserved for CW and narrowband digital modes; SSB (phone) can be used in a specific part of the band and is mostly active at night. Large loops or shortened verticals are common because the length needed for a full-size dipole is impractical for most amateur sites.

Spot frequencies #

1836 kHz is a centre of activity used as the gathering point for stations working CW QRP (low power).

Notes specific to Turkey #

The band is allocated to class A amateurs in Turkey. The TR table states that J3E (SSB) emission may be used only in the range 1832-1835 kHz; this restriction differs from the wider phone sub-band in the IARU Region 1 band plan, so care must be taken to stay within this narrow range when operating SSB from Turkey.
