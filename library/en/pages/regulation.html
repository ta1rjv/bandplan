Legal basis #

The current legal framework of amateur radio in Turkey is the BTK board decision "Technical Criteria for License-Exempt Radio Equipment and Systems" (2022/IK-SYD/245, dated 23.09.2022). [1] The basis of this decision is the FTM Regulation (Official Gazette 30608, 27.11.2018). [2]

The article of the Technical Criteria text that concerns amateur radio is ARTICLE 22 (MADDE 22). The source document has an internal numbering inconsistency: the amateur table is printed under the heading "Tablo 25" (Table 25), but the text of ARTICLE 22 refers to the same table as "Tablo-26", and the emission table appears as "Tablo 26-1". [1]

ARTICLE 22 - key points (summary) #

  • (9) Class A: May use all frequencies in the table, within the conditions of the table. [1]
  • (10) Class B and C supervised operation:

    Class B and C operators, while operating under the responsible operator's supervision at a club, school, or research station, or at a class A station, may use ALL table frequencies and emission types. Outside that context, they may only use the frequencies permitted for their own class.

    [1]
  • (11) Unlicensed participants: May be present at the station for training purposes, under the supervision of the responsible operator. [1]
  • (12) Repeaters and internet-radio interfaces: Repeaters may be installed by amateur radio associations; experimental work and internet-radio interface activities (e.g. EchoLink) are carried out in accordance with the principles of BTK frequency planning. [1]
  • (13) Encryption ban: Encryption is prohibited; open (unencrypted) protocols such as Q codes, Morse, RTTY, SSTV, FSTV, PSK, PACTOR, AMPR and APRS may be used. [1]

Note: Some paragraphs of ARTICLE 22 that are not summarized on this page are outside the scope of this site.

KEGM examination regulation cross-reference note #

The KEGM (Directorate General of Coastal Safety) Amateur Radio Examination Regulation (Official Gazette 27482, 03.02.2010) [3] still refers to ANNEX-4 Table-1 of the 2009 Private Radio Systems Regulation, which was repealed by the FTM Regulation in 2018. [2] This is a cross-reference gap between the examination legislation and the current band plan legislation; this site is based on the current (2022) table.
