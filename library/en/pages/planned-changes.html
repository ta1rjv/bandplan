WRC-23: 1240-1300 MHz (23 cm) and RNSS protection #

Verified At WRC-23 (Dubai, December 2023) a new Radio Regulations footnote was added to the amateur/amateur-satellite allocation in the 1240-1300 MHz band (with the provisional number 5.A91B in the WRC-23 document; the permanent RR footnote number could not be verified in this research round). The footnote requires that administrations and amateurs do not cause harmful interference to RNSS (radionavigation-satellite service, space-to-Earth) receivers; no change was made to the allocation table and amateur access continues. [1]

With the same decision, Resolution 774 of WRC-19 (the resolution that started a four-year ITU-R study on this subject) was suppressed. [1]

Verified Within CEPT, a regional study specific to the Galileo (RNSS) system is still ongoing (this is an ongoing process, not a completed WRC-23 outcome). [1]

WRC-27 (2027 World Radiocommunication Conference) #

Secondary source WRC-27 will be held in Shanghai, China, from 18 October to 12 November 2027. [2] The decision that sets the agenda is Resolution 813, adopted at WRC-23. [3]

From the direct text of Resolution 813 (Res. 813), the verified scope/relationship for the agenda items that intersect with the amateur service is listed below:

Item Scope (summary) Amateur relationship Confidence level
1.8 Additional primary allocation to the radiolocation service in the 231.5-275 GHz range Directly overlaps the IARU amateur 241 GHz band (241-250 GHz). [3] Verified
1.10 Power flux-density limits for FSS/MSS/BSS at 71-76 GHz and 81-86 GHz Adjacent to the amateur 75.5-81.5 GHz range; no direct overlap is stated. [3] Verified
1.15 Space research service communications between the lunar surface and lunar orbit and between lunar orbit and the lunar surface The claim that 430-440 MHz is left outside the scope of this item is supported only by secondary (IARU/ARRL summary) sources. [3] Secondary source
1.17 Regulatory provisions for receive-only space weather sensors The claim that the amateur HF bands and 28/50 MHz are left outside the scope is supported only by secondary sources. [3] Secondary source

Research gap: Items named in some secondary sources, such as 1300-1350 MHz and the 10 GHz/3.3 GHz IMT expansion, could not be found in the primary text of Resolution 813; these item numbers have not been added to the site because they could not be verified.

IARU Region 1: decisions after the Paris Interim Meeting (April 2025) #

Verified According to the Executive Committee (EC) minutes dated 21 May 2025: [4]

  • Restructuring of the 23 cm band: It was decided to set up a working group for the re-allocation of the 23 cm band; only "the next General Conference" (the 27th General Conference, Vienna, September 2026) is given as the target delivery date, no exact date is given. [4]
  • Phase-out of 1.3 GHz FMATV use: It was decided to phase out FMATV (frequency-modulated amateur television) use in the range 1258-1300 MHz by December 2026; national-level options will be investigated for coordinated DATV repeater outputs directly above 1300 MHz. [4]
  • Digital voice on the HF bands: It was recommended, and approved by the EC, that digital voice activity be located in the same "all-mode" segments as other voice modes such as SSB/AM/FM. [4]
  • HAREC licence syllabus review: A working group was set up to update the CEPT HAREC amateur radio licence syllabus, and the work was requested to be completed by 15 January 2026 (this is a decision on the training/examination syllabus, not a band plan). [4]

At this meeting no concrete boundary (frequency range) change in the HF or VHF/UHF/microwave band plans was adopted; all of the items above are either a harmonization recommendation, a preliminary-study recommendation, or the setting up of a working group aimed at the 2026 General Conference.

Verified As of this site's data collection date (2026-09-25), there has been no officially published update to the IARU Region 1 HF band plan since 16 October 2020. [5]

IARU Region 1, 27th General Conference (Vienna, September 2026) #

Verified The date/location of the conference is verified; however, as of this site's data collection date the decision/recommendation texts of the conference concerning band plans (including the proposal of the 23 cm working group) have not yet been published. [6]

Research gap: The technical/band plan outcomes of the Vienna 2026 conference will not appear on this site until IARU Region 1 publishes the official minutes.

Threat to the 70 cm band: AST SpaceMobile TT&C use #

Verified According to the October 2025 meeting summary of the IARU Administrative Council, AST & Science LLC (AST SpaceMobile) wants to use the 430-440 MHz amateur and amateur-satellite band for telemetry, tracking and command (TT&C) purposes for its system that aims to provide voice communication directly to handheld devices via low-Earth-orbit satellites. The FCC Space Bureau granted the company a licence for this band on 29 August 2025; however, the licence covers only 20 additional satellites instead of the 243 requested and limits use to the launch and early orbit phase (LEOP) and to unusual satellite operating situations, for a period of at most 24 hours. The IARU considers the use of ITU Radio Regulations Article 4.4 on this matter inappropriate. [6]

Unverified Research gap: In a previous research round it was found that the RSGB submitted an input document titled "VI26_C5_11 UHF Spectrum Situation" for the Vienna 2026 conference, addressing the same subject. However, this document has not been added to this site's bibliography and it could not be verified whether it was adopted at the conference.

Planned changes in Turkey (BTK) #

Research gap: In the research carried out within this project, no publicly available announcement/decision/consultation text was found concerning a change planned by BTK in the amateur band plan (60 m, 4 m, 13 cm, power limits, etc.) for 2023-2026. This is not evidence that there is no change - it only means that no evidence was found in this research round; therefore no claim is published on the site.
